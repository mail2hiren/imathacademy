1. How it all fits together

One rule runs through the whole system: the curriculum decides, everything else obeys.

The Curriculum Designer is the top of the chain. What you set there flows outward without anyone editing code:

  • It tells the LX Designer what a worksheet at each level may contain — how many digits, how large the numbers, which operations are permitted.
  • It tells the student app whether a child works on the abacus or in their head.
  • It defines what finishing a level requires.
  • Its level names are what children see on their dashboard.
What this means in practice If a worksheet comes out wrong, the fix is usually in the Curriculum Designer, not the LX Designer. Change the level rule once and every future worksheet at that level obeys it.

2. The Curriculum Designer

Admin → Curriculum Designer. Nine levels, L0 through L8, each with five tabs.

Tab 1 — Level Definition

The identity of the level and its number rules. Every one of these is passed to the AI when a worksheet is generated, so they are guardrails rather than notes.

SettingWhat it controls
Level nameWhat children see: "Level 1 · Foundation"
Core focusShown on the child's progress card
Age rangeGuidance only
Min / max digitsHard limit on how long the numbers may be
Min / max numberHard limit on the values themselves
Min / max rowsHow many numbers appear in one sum
Allow zeroWhether 0 may appear as a term or an answer

Abacus Mode & Operations

The same card sets which operations are allowed and how the abacus behaves:

  • Physical abacus — required, optional or not_used
  • Anzan allowed — children may solve mentally
  • Multiplication, division, decimals, negative numbers — each on or off
One thing to confirm When Anzan is switched on, the app currently allows mental solving only for concepts you have marked Mastered. If you meant that anything at that level may be solved mentally, say so and it will be changed — it is a one-line difference.

Tab 2 — Concept Progression

For each concept at this level, where the child is in learning it. This is what decides whether the abacus guides them or lets them work:

StatusMeaningWhat the child gets
I IntroducedBeing taught nowGuided abacus
P PractisedWorking on itAbacus, they set the answer
M MasteredConfidentMay work mentally, if Anzan is on
R RevisionComing back to itAbacus
N Not applicableNot at this levelNever appears

Tab 3 — Difficulty Rules

Easy, Medium and Hard within the same level. Anything set here overrides the level definition — so Hard at L2 can use bigger numbers than Easy at L2, while both stay inside L2.

Tab 4 — Formula Master

The actual abacus rules you teach at this level, in your own words. These go into the AI prompt, so worksheets use the formulas you teach rather than generic arithmetic.

Tab 5 — Guardrails

Three switches for what is forbidden, plus a free-text box. Whatever you write in Additional Guardrail Notes is passed to the AI word for word, under a heading telling it these rules override everything else.

The most powerful box in the system Write plainly. "Never use carrying at this level." "Only single-digit answers." "Word problems must be about cricket or food." It is read as an instruction, not a suggestion.

Completion criteria

Two further settings decide what finishing this level requires — how many practice sessions, and the pass mark on the level test. Defaults are 20 sessions and 80%.

3. LX Designer — making worksheets

Teacher → LX Designer. Every sum is built from the curriculum and checked against the beads before you ever see it. Nothing is written by AI any more.

What changed Worksheets used to be written by an AI, which did not know what a bead can physically do — that is how a Level 1 child once got 9 + 2 inside a story about a superhero. All nine activities are now generated from your curriculum, and every sum is walked step by step against that level's rules. Anything that breaks them is rebuilt before the sheet reaches you.
  1. Pick a level. This loads that level's rules — number range, row counts, and which formulas are permitted.
  2. Pick a concept. Anything dimmed is not taught at this level.
  3. Pick an activity. All nine build instantly, with no waiting.
  4. Settings. Difficulty, question count, theme, abacus mode, restrictions, hand-in date.
  5. Preview — See it as the child will steps through the questions in the child's own layout.
  6. Assign to a batch, one child, or several chosen children.

What each level produces

The same activity gives very different work depending on the level, because the level decides what a child is allowed to do with the beads:

LevelA typical column sumWhy
L0 Beginner 21 + 1 − 21
17 − 7 + 12		 Direct bead movement only. No Big or Small Friends anywhere — your Level 0 note asks for exactly this.
L1 Foundation 2 + 18  (Big Friends)
3 + 3 − 6  (Small Friends)		 Every sum requires the formula being taught. A sheet labelled Big Friends really practises Big Friends.
L3 Pre-Intermediate 22 − 16 − 6 + 14 Forces the Combination formula — where the ten is needed and the five must be broken, as in your 8 + 6 example.
L4 Intermediate 13 × 9, 22 × 4 2 digit × 1 digit, taken from your own Level 4 exam paper.
L5 Upper-Intermediate 341 × 8  ·  981 ÷ 9 3 × 1 and 3 ÷ 1. Division is always exact — no remainders.
L6 Advanced 44 × 23  ·  6335 ÷ 5 Cross-multiplication and 4 ÷ 1, matching your Jr7 paper.

A page gets harder as it goes

Question 1 is not question 20. A Level 0 sheet opens with three rows of small numbers and finishes with five rows of two-digit work:

 Sum
Q12 + 2 − 1
Q68 + 11 − 1 − 5
Q1331 + 18 − 29 − 10
Q2028 + 21 − 37 + 71 + 5

Every answer on a page is different, as your Level 0 note asks.

The nine activities

ActivityWhat a child gets
Column sums The classic column, printed as in the books.
Story problems "Aarav had 4 balloons. He was given 15 balloons. Then he gave away 9 balloons. How many balloons are left?" Read aloud for the younger children.
Fill in the blank 30 + ___ = 50 — the strongest exercise at Level 1, because a child cannot guess it; they have to use the formula.
Match pairs Five sums and five answers to pair up. Answers are always distinct.
Picture sums The starting number shown as beads rather than digits.
Colour the answer Sums with a colour key — 0–16 red, 17–33 blue.
Flash cards / Speed challenge Quick recall against the clock.
Mixed Draws from all of the above.

Restricting a sheet

Under the settings you can narrow what a sheet contains. These are obeyed exactly — a sum that breaks one is rebuilt, not just flagged:

SettingUse it when
Addition onlyA child is new to the level and subtraction is a distraction
Subtraction onlyDrilling the subtraction formulas specifically
Never land on zeroZero totals confuse some children mid-column
Keep totals under …Pulling a sheet below the level's normal range for a child who is struggling
Replace a single sum Every question in the preview has a ↻ Replace button. It swaps that one sum for another of the same shape and leaves the rest of the sheet — and any edits you have made — exactly as they are.

Giving it to several children

Under the Student dropdown, + Choose several children opens a tick list. Each child gets their own copy, so their marks stay separate.

Each child at their own level With Pitch each child's copy to their own level ticked, one click gives a beginner sums in the 9–20 range and a child near the end of the level sums in the 40–70 range — same activity, same concept, different difficulty. Untick it if you want the whole group on the identical sheet for a class discussion.

The Abacus dropdown

This overrides the level for this worksheet only — so you can set one sheet on the beads and the next as a mental drill, at the same level, in the same week.

ChoiceEffect
Follow the levelUses the curriculum setting
Required — beads onlyAbacus shown, typing locked
OptionalAbacus shown, typing also allowed
Mental onlyNo abacus — an Anzan drill

Hand-in date and time

Both are optional and neither is enforced. A child sees "Due in 3 days" and "About 10 minutes", but nothing locks and nothing is cut off — a child working through a sum should never lose it to a clock.

If a sheet looks wrong Every generated sheet carries a line confirming it was checked — "Every sum checked against the beads — direct bead movement only". If that line is red, the level's own settings are asking for something impossible, and the fix is in the Curriculum Designer.

Worksheets made before August 2026 keep the sums they were created with. Fixing the curriculum does not rewrite them — retire them from How the class is doing → Worksheets → Remove.

4. How the abacus behaves

A 17-rod soroban, built to your specification, used as the way children answer.

  • Rod 9 is the ones. Rod 8 tens, rod 7 hundreds, continuing leftward.
  • Rod 10 is the first decimal, rod 11 the second, continuing rightward.
  • Unit dots on the divider at rods 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15.
  • Beads slide along the rod. The value updates as the child moves them.

Where it appears

Inside a worksheetBelow the check button. Moving beads fills the answer box.

6. Rewards

Four systems, deliberately different from each other.

XP

Earned from every activity. Drives the level-up ceremony. Champions are ranked on it weekly. Hidden from Tiny Champs — a number means little at five.

Badges

Rare, one of each, for real achievements: Speed Star, Perfect Score, 5-Day Fire, Puzzle Pro, Top of Batch, Abacus Ninja. Shown to Rising Stars and Champions.

Stickers

The opposite of badges — frequent, and duplicates are the point. A child likes having three cats. Twenty-four to collect across three tiers.

Earned byTierLimit
Finishing a worksheetCommonOne per worksheet, ever
Full marks on a worksheetRareInstead of the common one
Keeping the streakCommonOne a day
Levelling upShinyOnce per level

Redoing a worksheet earns nothing — the limit is enforced in the database, not just the app.

Puzzles

Not a menu item. A child unlocks one by getting five practice questions right in a row, and it is offered there and then.

Age groupPuzzle
Tiny ChampsMaze
Rising StarsSudoku 4×4
ChampionsSudoku 9×9
A gap worth knowing Practice and puzzles award XP and badges, but no stickers. Since Tiny Champs have no XP bar and stickers are their only visible reward, practice currently looks like nothing happened to them. This is being fixed.

7. Finishing a level

Three things, all three required, then the certificate.

  1. Every worksheet for the level submitted.
  2. Enough practice sessions. You set the number per level.
  3. A pass on the level test. You set the mark per level.

Only then does the child earn the certificate and start the next level. Certificates carry a real number — IMA-L1-000042 — and only a teacher or admin can award one.

Partly built The three checks work and the certificate records exist. What is not built yet is the screen showing a child their progress toward these three, and the certificate as something to print.

11. Seeing how children did

Teacher → How the class is doing. Scores tell you a child struggled; this tells you with what.

Every child, at a glance

Each child shows their practice count and accuracy, worksheets done and average, and quizzes with their average. Anything worth acting on is said in words rather than left in the numbers:

  • "Nothing for 9 days — worth a message home"
  • "Getting 45% in practice — the level may be too hard"
  • "92% in practice — ready for harder work"
That last one matters as much as the warnings. A child quietly finding it too easy is invisible in a table of green numbers.

Which sums went wrong

The Worksheets tab lists what has been assigned. Marks on any worksheet shows every child against every question — a green tick for right, and for a wrong answer what the child actually wrote.

Below that, each question is written out with the answer, how many got it, and who missed it:

Q3 · most of the class missed this    2/5 got it
19 − 2 − 7 − 10    Answer: 0
Aarav said 12 · Diya said 4 · Kabir said 30
Not attempted: Meera

If three children all answered 12 to that sum, they have made the same mistake — probably stopping after the first subtraction. That is one thing to reteach at the board, not three children to worry about.

Taking a worksheet back

Remove takes a worksheet off the children's list immediately. Nothing is deleted, so it can be put back.

One attempt per question A child answers each question once and it stands. Until August 2026 a wrong answer said "try again" and the retry overwrote the first — so every worksheet came back at 100% and told you nothing. Marks from before that date are not reliable.

12. Each child's own pace within a level

Teacher → Pacing. Two children on Level 2 are rarely doing the same work. This is where you see and steer that.

Every child sits somewhere within their level, from newly arrived to working at its full range. Practice aims at that spot rather than at the whole level, so a child on their first day never meets the hardest sum the level allows.

Where they areNumbers they getRows
First week9–163
Mid-level25–524
Nearly ready for the next level48–995

It moves on its own

After each practice session the position shifts on what the child actually did:

They scoredPosition moves
90% or betterUp a step
70–90%Up a small step
Below 70%Back down, gently

Down is deliberately slower than up — a tired afternoon should not undo a fortnight.

Setting a floor

Use this for a child you know is capable Drag the slider to where they should be, then press Set floor here. The position can still rise, but it will never drop below that mark however one bad session goes. The numbers update as you drag, so you are choosing a range of sums rather than a score.
This is not how a child moves up a level Pacing decides what they practise today. Moving from Level 2 to Level 3 is the three gates in section 7 — worksheets, practice sessions, and the level test.

13. The learning journey

What a child sees on their dashboard, and what it is for.

The path of levels

A scrollable row of circles — one per level. Completed levels show a tick, the current level is highlighted, levels ahead are locked. It centres on where they are.

The youngest children see a window of four levels rather than all nine. Nine locked circles is discouraging when you are five.

Inside the current level

Tapping the current level opens the concepts being taught there, each with a standing:

StandingMeaning
Just startedNewly introduced
PractisingBeing worked on now
MasteredSolid; appears only as revision
RevisingKept warm from an earlier level

These come straight from your Concept Progression. What you mark in the Curriculum Designer is what the child sees.

Road to the certificate

A block on the dashboard showing the three gates and what is still outstanding — worksheets done, practice sessions completed, and the level test. For the youngest it is headed "How to finish this level" rather than talking about certificates.

It always names one next step rather than listing everything remaining. A child who needs four more practice sessions is told that, not shown three unfinished bars.

14. XP, stickers, badges and puzzles

Four different things, doing four different jobs. Worth knowing which is which when a child asks you about them.

XP — effort

Points for doing the work, awarded per worksheet, practice session and quiz, scaled by how well it went. XP never goes down. It is the quiet running total behind a child's progress.

Stickers — a collection

Frequent, and duplicates are welcome. Roughly one per session. There are 24 in three tiers:

TierHow manyWhen
Common12 — Kitty, Puppy, Panda, Froggy…Most sessions
Rare7A perfect worksheet, a streak
Shiny5Finishing a level, a real milestone
Why duplicates are allowed A five-year-old is motivated by a collection that visibly grows. Children like having three cats. Badges are the opposite — rare, one of each, "you achieved something".

Puzzles — a reward, not a lesson

Sudoku, mazes and pattern games, unlocked by doing the work. They are deliberately not abacus practice. A child who has finished their sums has earned something that is simply fun.

Streaks

Consecutive days of practice. Useful, but watch for a child chasing the streak with token one-minute sessions — the pacing screen will show accuracy falling while the streak climbs.

15. A week in the life — the whole flow

If you are new, this is the shape of it.

  1. Before class — open How the class is doing. The flags tell you who has gone quiet and who is finding it too easy.
  2. Make the sheet — LX Designer, pick the level and concept, choose Column sums or another activity, and preview it as the child will see it.
  3. Assign it — to the batch, or tick the three children who need something different. Leave pitch to their own level ticked so each gets their own range.
  4. During the week — children work on the abacus at home; practice adapts to each child on its own.
  5. After — open Marks on that worksheet. Look at the question breakdown before the individual scores: a question most of the class missed is a lesson to reteach, not five children to worry about.
  6. Adjust — a child stuck at 45%? Set their pacing floor lower, or send a sheet with Addition only ticked. A child at 95%? They are ready for harder work.
  7. When they are ready — the three gates light up and you can award the certificate.
The one habit worth forming Read the question breakdown before the child breakdown. Most of what looks like a struggling child is a sum the whole class found hard.
