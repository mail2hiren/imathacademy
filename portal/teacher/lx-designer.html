📖 Worksheet Generation Guide
Step by step — how to generate a worksheet
1
Select student or batch
Choose which batch this worksheet is for. If you want a worksheet for one specific student, select their name from the student dropdown. The level will auto-fill based on the student's current level.
2
Choose level and concept
The level is usually set automatically from the student. The concept is what you are practising today — Big Friends, Small Friends, Combination etc. Only concepts valid for the selected level will be available.
3
Choose Activity Type
This controls how questions are presented. Flash Cards for quick drills. Story Problems for word problems. See the Activity Types section below for full details.
4
Set difficulty, count, theme and mascot
Difficulty controls complexity within the level. Question count is how many questions in the worksheet. Theme and mascot make the worksheet fun and personalised for your students.
5
Choose output sections
Tick what you need. Questions for Students is always on. Answer Key helps you mark. Hint Box helps students who are stuck. Lesson Plan gives you a ready class structure. Parent Note goes home with the child.
6
Generate and assign
Tap Generate with AI. The worksheet appears in 10-15 seconds. Review it — if you are happy, tap Assign to send it to the student app. Students see it in their Homework and Worksheets tabs.
Activity types explained
⚡ Flash Cards
Short, direct calculations. One sum per card. Best for warm-up drills and speed practice. No story, no context — just the maths.
"43 + 28 = ?"  ·  "56 - 17 = ?"
📖 Story Problems
Word problems with Indian names, settings and characters. The mascot you choose becomes the main character. Great for developing reading comprehension alongside maths. Every question has a complete story and ends with "How many...?"
"Tiger had 9 cricket balls. Aryan gave him 8 more. How many cricket balls does Tiger have now?"
✏️ Fill in the Blank
Questions with a missing number shown as ___. The blank can be the answer, a missing operand, or a missing operator. Good for testing understanding rather than just calculation.
"43 + ___ = 71"  ·  "___ + 28 = 71"  ·  "43 + 28 = ___"
🔗 Match the Pairs
Column A has expressions, Column B has answers. Students draw a line to match. Includes some distractors in Column B. Good for assessment.
Column A: "9 + 8" → Column B: "17"
⏱️ Speed Challenge
Numbered rapid-fire questions. Slightly simpler than normal to allow fast solving. Great for timed practice. Shows time limit prominently.
"Q1: 9 + 8 = ?"  ·  "Q2: 7 + 6 = ?"
🖼️ Visual Picture Sums
Questions use emojis to represent objects visually. Great for young children 5-7 years who enjoy pictures. The emoji count matches the actual numbers.
"🏏🏏🏏🏏 + 🏏🏏🏏 = ?" (4 + 3 = ?)
🎨 Colour by Answer
Each question has a colour assigned. Student solves the sum and colours the section that colour. Makes a picture when completed. Most fun for 5-8 year olds.
"9 + 8 = ? → Colour BLUE"  ·  "7 + 6 = ? → Colour RED"
🎲 Mixed Variety
A mix of direct calculation (30%), fill in the blank (30%) and story problems (40%). All questions test the same concept. Good for a well-rounded practice session.
Mix of all formats above, all testing the same concept
Output options — what each one gives you
📝
Questions for Students
Always included. This is the worksheet the student sees and solves in the app.
🔑
Answer Key
Lists all answers with one line showing the method. Useful for marking and for explaining to students how to solve each question using the correct formula.
💡
Hint / Formula Box
A short, child-friendly reminder of the key rule for the selected concept. Printed at the top of the worksheet. Great for students who are still learning the formula.
🗂️
Lesson Plan
A 5-step class plan: warm-up, concept intro, guided practice, independent work, wrap-up. Ready to use — saves preparation time.
👨‍👩‍👧
Parent Note
3 sentences for the parent: what was practised, one home activity to try tonight, and an encouraging word. Send home with the student's book.
Settings explained
🎯
Difficulty
Difficulty stays within the selected level.Easy = simpler numbers, fewer rows. Hard = larger numbers, more rows. Hard Level 2 never becomes Level 3 — it stays within Level 2 boundaries.
Easy, Medium or Hard — all within the selected level. Hard at Level 2 stays within Level 2 rules. It never jumps to Level 3 concepts.
🔢
Question Count
How many questions in the worksheet. 5-10 for younger children. 10-15 for older students. 20 for a full test.
⏰
Time Limit
Shown on the worksheet header. Tells the student how long they have. Does not enforce a timer — just sets the expectation.
🎨
Theme
The overall setting of the worksheet.Every question fits this theme. Try: Cricket, Diwali, Space, Jungle, Ocean, Superheroes, Cooking, Monsoon.
Tip: Change theme every week — same concept stays fresh!
The overall context and setting of the worksheet. Every question will fit this theme. Good themes: Cricket, Diwali, Space, Jungle, Ocean, Superheroes, Cooking.
🐯
Mascot
A character who appears in every question.For Story Problems: the mascot is the hero. For Flash Cards: the mascot introduces the set. Use the same mascot for a student every week — they will love it!
Good mascots: Tiger, Elephant, Monkey, Robot, Wizard
A character who appears throughout the worksheet. For story problems the mascot is the main character. For flash cards the mascot introduces the set. Children connect with a familiar mascot across multiple worksheets.
Good mascots: Tiger, Elephant, Monkey, Robot, Wizard, Dragon, Superhero
🌐
Language
The language all questions will be written in. English is default. Select Hindi or Gujarati for regional language worksheets.

Tips for the best worksheets
✅
Match the activity to the age
Story Problems and Colour by Answer work best for 5-8 year olds. Flash Cards and Speed Challenge work better for 9-12 year olds.
✅
Use a consistent mascot per student
If a student loves Tiger, always use Tiger for their worksheets. They will look forward to seeing their mascot each time.
✅
Change the theme every week
Same concept, different theme keeps practice fresh. Week 1: Cricket. Week 2: Diwali. Week 3: Space. Week 4: Jungle.
✅
Use Special Instructions for struggling students
If a student finds a concept hard, add "Keep numbers small and simple" and reduce question count to 5.
✅
Always check the Answer Key before assigning
AI is accurate but not perfect. Quickly check the answers before sending to students.
✅
Use Lesson Plan for new teachers
The Lesson Plan output gives a complete class structure. Share it with new teachers joining iMathAcademy.
