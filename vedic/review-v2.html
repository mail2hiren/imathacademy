LEVEL 1 · about 2 months

Foundation

Complements, doubling, and quick addition and subtraction
By the end: Take any number from 100 or 1000 and their multiples instantly. Add and subtract two-digit numbers. Double and halve with confidence.

Taking any number away from 100, 1000, or a multiple of them

All from 9, and the last from 10
Nikhilam Navatashcaramam Dashatah
When a child can use it
Whenever you subtract from a base — 100, 1000. Every digit except the last comes off 9; the last comes off 10.
When they should not
Not for ordinary subtraction like 47 − 23, where neither number is a base.
How it is taught
1000 − 357 9 − 3 = 6 9 − 5 = 4 10 − 7 = 3 Answer 643 For a multiple such as 300 − 176, take 176 from 100 and add the 200.
Works well on
1000 - 357 = 643
100 - 68 = 32
300 - 176 = 124
2000 - 1481 = 519
Should not be used on
47 - 23
856 - 341
Common mistake
Taking the last digit from 9 as well, giving 642 instead of 643.
Rule for the app: Bases are 100 and 1000 and their multiples at this level — not 10000 yet.

Finding the complement — what a number needs to reach the next ten or hundred

Friends / Param Mitra
Purana-Apuranabhyam
When a child can use it
Any single or two-digit number, finding what it needs to make 10, 100 or 1000.
When they should not
Not needed once a child adds fluently without thinking in complements.
How it is taught
What does 68 need to make 100? All from 9, last from 10: 3 and 2 → 32
Works well on
68 → 100 = 32
7 → 10 = 3
450 → 1000 = 550
Common mistake
Forgetting that a number ending in 0 takes its last digit from 10 as well, so 450 → 550 not 560.
Rule for the app: Complements underpin everything later. A child should be quick at this before any multiplication method.

Doubling and halving any number

Doubling
Anurupyena (proportionately)
When a child can use it
Any number. Doubling repeatedly gives ×4 and ×8; halving gives ÷2 and ÷4.
When they should not
Halving an odd number introduces a half, which should wait until decimals.
How it is taught
Double 47 Double the tens: 80 Double the units: 14 80 + 14 = 94
Works well on
double 47 = 94
double 68 = 136
half of 86 = 43
Should not be used on
half of 47
Common mistake
Doubling the digits separately and writing 814 instead of carrying.
Rule for the app: Only give two digit numbers for halving at this level.

Adding and subtracting by completing to a ten

Split and merge
Ekadhikena Purvena
When a child can use it
Adding a number close to a ten or hundred — add the round number, then adjust.
When they should not
Not worth it when both numbers are already easy, like 23 + 14.
How it is taught
68 + 27 Add 30: 98 Take back 3: 95
Works well on
68 + 27 = 95
145 + 98 = 243
76 - 29 = 47
Should not be used on
23 + 14
Common mistake
Adjusting the wrong way — adding 3 back instead of taking it off.
Rule for the app: The number added should be within 3 of a ten.

Adding two-digit numbers by stackingNEW

Stacking
When a child can use it
Simple addition — 2 digit plus 1 digit, and 2 digit plus 2 digit — written one under the other.
When they should not
Not for long columns; that is abacus work.
How it is taught
47 + 8 —— 55
Works well on
47 + 8 = 55
36 + 52 = 88
68 + 27 = 95
Common mistake
Forgetting the carry from the units column.
Rule for the app: Two numbers only, of two digits or fewer. The plain method, before the shortcuts.

Checking an answer with digit sumsNEW

Digit sum
Navasesh (casting out nines)
When a child can use it
Any addition or multiplication. Reduce each number to a single digit and the check must agree.
When they should not
It shows an answer is wrong, never that it is certainly right.
How it is taught
234 × 3 = 702 Digit sums: 234 → 9, 3 → 3, 9 × 3 = 27 → 9 702 → 9. They agree.
Works well on
234 x 3 = 702 → 9 and 9
47 + 58 = 105 → 6 and 6
Common mistake
Believing the answer must be right because the check passed.
Rule for the app: A way of catching a mistake, not of proving correctness.

LEVEL 3 · about 2 months

Multiplication I

The general method, and the special ones that beat it
By the end: Multiply any two two-digit numbers in one line, square anything ending in 5, and choose sensibly between methods.

Multiplying any two numbers, whatever they are

Vertically and crosswise
Urdhva-Tiryagbhyam
When a child can use it
Any two numbers of any size. The general method that always works.
When they should not
It always applies — but when a special method fits, that one is quicker.
How it is taught
23 × 41 Units: 3 × 1 = 3 Cross: (2×1)+(3×4) = 14 → write 4 carry 1 Tens: 2×4 = 8, plus 1 = 9 Answer 943
Works well on
23 x 41 = 943
36 x 74 = 2664
52 x 63 = 3276
47 x 58 = 2726
Common mistake
Losing a carry from the crosswise step.
Rule for the app: Introduce without carrying, then one carry, then freely.

Multiplying a three-digit number by a two-digit numberNEW

Three by two
Urdhva-Tiryagbhyam
When a child can use it
Any three-digit number times a two-digit one, by the same crosswise pattern.
When they should not
—
How it is taught
234 × 12 The same crosswise steps, four of them instead of three.
Works well on
234 x 12 = 2808
512 x 34 = 17408
407 x 23 = 9361

Missing one of the middle crosswise pairs.
Rule for the app: Only after two by two is fluent.

Squaring any number ending in 5

One more than the one before
Ekadhikena Purvena
When a child can use it
Only numbers ending in 5, being squared. 25², 65², 115².
When they should not
Not for numbers ending in anything else, and not for two different numbers.
How it is taught
65² Take the 6, multiply by one more: 6 × 7 = 42 Write 25 after it Answer 4225
Works well on
25 x 25 = 625
65 x 65 = 4225
95 x 95 = 9025
115 x 115 = 13225
Should not be used on
64 x 64
65 x 75
Common mistake
Using it for 65 × 75, where both end in 5 but are different numbers.
Rule for the app: Must end in 5 AND be squared. Never generate a sum where only one condition holds.

Multiplying two numbers with the same tens digit whose units add to 10

Same first, friends after
Antyayordashake'pi
When a child can use it
43 × 47 — same tens digit, units summing to 10. Also 62 × 68, 91 × 99.
When they should not
If the tens digits differ, or the units do not add to 10.
How it is taught
43 × 47 Tens digit 4, times one more: 4 × 5 = 20 Units: 3 × 7 = 21 Answer 2021
Works well on
43 x 47 = 2021
62 x 68 = 4216
91 x 99 = 9009
24 x 26 = 624
Should not be used on
43 x 57
43 x 46
Common mistake
Writing the units product as one digit — 91 × 99 needs 09.
Rule for the app: Both conditions exactly. The right-hand part always fills two digits.

Choosing which method fits a sum

Which method?
When a child can use it
Any multiplication. The child looks at the numbers and decides before starting.
When they should not
—
How it is taught
Ends in 5 and squared? One more than before. Same tens, units make 10? That method. Otherwise vertically and crosswise.
Works well on
65 x 65 → ekadhikena
43 x 47 → antyayor
36 x 74 → urdhva
Common mistake
Reaching for a special method when the numbers do not fit it.
Rule for the app: An exercise type in its own right — a mixed page where the child names the method first.

LEVEL 4 · about 2 months

Base multiplication

Multiplying near a base, below it and above it
By the end: Multiply any two numbers close to 100 or 1000, below or above the base, and near a working base such as 50.

Multiplying two numbers close to a baseNEW

Base method
Nikhilam Navatashcaramam Dashatah
When a child can use it
Both numbers close to a base — 100 works best. Below it (97 × 96), above it (104 × 105), or one of each.
When they should not
If either number is far from the base, this is more work than long multiplication.
How it is taught
97 × 96 Below 100 by 3 and 4 Cross-subtract: 97 − 4 = 93 Differences: 3 × 4 = 12 Answer 9312 104 × 105 Above 100 by 4 and 5 Cross-add: 104 + 5 = 109 Differences: 4 × 5 = 20 Answer 10920
Works well on
97 x 96 = 9312
88 x 91 = 8008
104 x 105 = 10920
112 x 103 = 11536
Should not be used on
43 x 61
23 x 41
Common mistake
Not padding the right-hand side — 98 × 97 gives 06, so 9506 not 956.
Rule for the app: Both numbers within about 12 of the base. The right part fills as many digits as the base has zeros.

Multiplying near a base that is not a power of ten

Working base
Anurupyena
When a child can use it
Numbers near 50, 200, 250, 500 — a convenient multiple of a power of ten.
When they should not
If no convenient working base is near, use vertically and crosswise.
How it is taught
48 × 47, working base 50 Differences −2 and −3 Cross-subtract: 48 − 3 = 45 Times the ratio: 45 × 5 = 225 Differences: 2 × 3 = 06 Answer 2256
Works well on
48 x 47 = 2256
52 x 54 = 2808
203 x 204 = 41412
Should not be used on
23 x 41
Common mistake
Forgetting to multiply the left part by the ratio between the working base and the power of ten.
Rule for the app: Both numbers within about 5 of the working base.

Squaring any two-digit number

Duplex
Dwandwa Yoga
When a child can use it
Squaring any number. The duplex of one digit is its square; of two digits, twice their product.
When they should not
For numbers ending in 5, the Level 3 method is quicker.
How it is taught
43² Duplex of 3 = 9 Duplex of 43 = 2 × 4 × 3 = 24 Duplex of 4 = 16 Assemble: 16 | 24 | 9 → 1849
Works well on
43 x 43 = 1849
67 x 67 = 4489
28 x 28 = 784
Should not be used on
65 x 65
Common mistake
Forgetting to double in the middle duplex.
Rule for the app: Avoid numbers ending in 5 — the Level 3 method is better and this teaches the wrong instinct.

By the end: Divide by numbers near a base, by numbers just above one, and by any number using the flag method.

Dividing by a number just below a base

Base division
Nikhilam
When a child can use it
Divisors just below 10, 100 or 1000 — 8, 9, 88, 97.
When they should not
Divisors above the base, or far from it.
How it is taught
1234 ÷ 88 Complement of 88 is 12 Bring down and add Quotient 14, remainder 2
Works well on
1234 / 88 = 14 r 2
1132 / 9 = 125 r 7
11220 / 89 = 126 r 6
Should not be used on
1234 / 43
Common mistake
Using the complement wrongly when the divisor is above the base.
Rule for the app: Divisor below the base and within about 12 of it.

Paravartya Yojayet

Divisors just above a base — 12, 13, 112.
When they should not
Divisors below the base, where base division is better.

1234 ÷ 12 Transpose 12 → −2 Bring down, multiply, add Quotient 102, remainder 10

Dividing by any number using a flag digit

Straight division
Dhwajanka (on top of the flag)
When a child can use it
Any divisor. The last digit becomes the flag and the rest divides.
When they should not
—
How it is taught
2695 ÷ 32 Flag the 2, divide by 3 Subtract the cross product each step Quotient 84, remainder 7

4321 / 21 = 205 r 16
1234 / 41 = 30 r 4

Roots

By the end: Find the square root and cube root of any perfect square or cube by inspection.

Finding the square root of a perfect square

Square roots
Vilokanam / Dwandwa Yoga
When a child can use it
Perfect squares. The last digit and the leading pair narrow it to one answer.
When they should not
Not for numbers that are not perfect squares — that needs the full duplex method.
How it is taught
√1849 Pairs: 18 | 49 Largest square under 18 is 16, so first digit 4 Last digit 9 → root ends in 3 or 7 43² = 1849, so 43
Works well on
root 1849 = 43
root 4489 = 67
root 7396 = 86
root 5776 = 76
Should not be used on
root 2000
Common mistake
Choosing the wrong ending when both candidates look plausible.
Rule for the app: Perfect squares only.

Finding the cube root of a perfect cube

Cube roots
Vilokanam
When a child can use it
Perfect cubes up to six digits. The last digit gives the last digit of the root directly.
When they should not
Not for numbers that are not perfect cubes.
How it is taught
∛12167 Last digit 7 → root ends in 3 First group 12 → between 2³ and 3³, so first digit 2 Answer 23
Works well on
cube root 12167 = 23
cube root 2744 = 14
cube root 39304 = 34
Common mistake
Forgetting that the last digits pair as 2↔8 and 3↔7.
Rule for the app: Perfect cubes only.

Solving a simple equation in one line

Transpose and apply
Paravartya Yojayet
When a child can use it
Linear equations, and those of the form (ax + b)/(cx + d) = e.
When they should not
Quadratics and above.
How it is taught
3x + 4 = 19 Transpose: x = (19 − 4)/3 = 5
Works well on
3x + 4 = 19 = x = 5
(x+1)/(x+3) = 1/2 = x = 1
Common mistake
Changing the sign of only one term when transposing.
Rule for the app: Linear equations only.

Spotting when the answer needs no working

When the totals match
Sunyam Samyasamuccaye
When a child can use it
When a term is common to both sides, or the totals of numerator and denominator match.
When they should not
When no such symmetry exists.
How it is taught
Where the sums of both sides are equal, x = 0. Where a factor is common to both, it cancels at once.
Works well on
(x+2)/(x+3) = (x+2)/(x+5) → x = -2
Common mistake
Applying it where the symmetry is only apparent.
Rule for the app: A recognition exercise, not a calculation one.
